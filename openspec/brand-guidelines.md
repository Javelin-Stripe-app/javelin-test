## 1. Brand Strategy & Positioning

### 1.1 Golden Circle (Why / How / What)

**Why:** Operators running their business on SaaS platforms are stuck between what the platform offers and what they actually need. The long tail of unsolved customization problems forces smart, capable people into dumb, manual workarounds. Javelin exists to close that gap -- to give non-technical operators the power to shape their tools to fit their business, not the other way around.

**How:** An AI-powered builder that lives inside the platform itself. It understands the user's data and workflows, takes plain-language instructions, and builds working solutions -- dashboards, automations, bulk operations -- instantly. No code. No setup. No separate tool.

**What:** A Stripe App that turns natural-language prompts into custom workflows, dashboards, and operational automations directly inside the Stripe Dashboard.

### 1.2 Positioning Statement

> **To** founders and ops leads at small, growing companies who run their business on Stripe,
> **Javelin** is the AI-powered builder inside your Stripe Dashboard
> **that** turns plain-language instructions into working workflows, dashboards, and automations --
> **because** every other tool in the Stripe ecosystem is a read-only analyst. Javelin is the only one that builds.

[insight] This positioning occupies an empty quadrant in the Stripe ecosystem: **in-dashboard AND read-write**. Every competitor (ChartMogul, Baremetrics, Coefficient, QuantLedger) is either outside the dashboard, read-only, or both. The "builder, not analyst" distinction is Javelin's core brand differentiator and must be reinforced at every touchpoint.

### 1.3 Category Definition

Javelin is defining a new micro-category: **"in-platform AI builder."** It is not:

- A BI/analytics tool (read-only, external)
- A Stripe integration (payment processing)
- A no-code platform (generic, standalone)
- A chatbot or AI assistant (passive, advisory)

It is closer to "what Notion is for documents" or "what Figma is for design" -- but applied to SaaS platform customization. The operating frame is: **AI that builds things for you inside the tools you already use.**

### 1.4 Brand Essence

**Javelin = Precision Power for Operators**

The brand essence distills into three words: **direct, capable, immediate.** Javelin does not deliberate, analyze, or advise endlessly. It understands what you need and builds it. The name itself encodes this: a javelin is a weapon of precision and directness -- it goes exactly where you aim it.

### 1.5 Core Values (Operating Principles)

These are behavioral -- the kind of thing you would fire someone for violating, not aspirational wall art.

1. **Ship, don't theorize.** Speed over polish, always. If it works, ship it. Optimize when it breaks. (Aligns with Pillar 1.)
2. **Respect the operator.** The user is smart and busy. Never condescend, never hide behind jargon, never make them feel like they need to be a developer. (Aligns with Pillar 2.)
3. **Go deep, not wide.** Master the platform you are in before chasing the next one. Depth earns trust. Breadth without depth is a party trick. (Aligns with Pillar 3.)
4. **Be the platform, not the bolt-on.** Javelin should feel like Stripe got smarter, not like a third-party tool got installed. (Aligns with Pillar 4.)
5. **Say what you did and why.** Transparency is non-negotiable. Every action is explained, logged, and reversible. Trust is earned through visibility, not promises.

## 2. Brand Archetype & Personality

### 2.1 Primary Archetype: The Magician

[insight] The Magician archetype is the strongest fit for Javelin. The Magician transforms reality -- it takes something complex and makes it feel effortless. "Describe what you want and it appears" is a fundamentally Magician promise. This aligns with Javelin's core value proposition: turning plain-language descriptions into working solutions.

**Magician traits that map to Javelin:**
- Transforms complexity into simplicity
- Makes the impossible feel achievable
- Operates through understanding, not brute force
- Delivers tangible outcomes, not just insights
- Inspires confidence through competence

**What the Magician is NOT (and Javelin should avoid):**
- Mystical or vague -- Javelin's magic is transparent and explainable
- Dramatic or grandiose -- Javelin is quietly effective, not theatrical
- Elitist -- Javelin's power is accessible to anyone, not reserved for experts

### 2.2 Secondary Archetype: The Sage (Supporting)

The Sage tempers the Magician with trustworthiness and reliability. In an AI product that modifies real business data, users need to trust what the system is doing. The Sage influence manifests as:

- Clear explanations of what was built and why
- Data freshness indicators and audit trails
- Honest about limitations ("I'll ask rather than guess wrong")
- Competent, measured tone rather than hype

### 2.3 Brand Personality (If Javelin Were a Person)

Javelin is the colleague who, when you describe a problem, says "I can fix that" -- and actually does, right then. Not the one who schedules a meeting to discuss it. Not the one who sends you a dashboard and says "the data is all there." The one who listens, understands, builds, and hands you the working solution with a brief explanation of what they did.

**Personality adjectives (ranked):**
1. **Direct** -- says what it means, does what it says
2. **Capable** -- quietly competent, never needs to boast
3. **Attentive** -- understands context and nuance without being told twice
4. **Trustworthy** -- transparent about what it did, honest about what it cannot do

**Personality anti-patterns (what Javelin is NOT):**
- Not playful or whimsical (this is serious business tooling, not a consumer app)
- Not corporate or stiff (this is for founders, not procurement committees)
- Not aggressive or competitive (no "crush the competition" energy)
- Not humble to a fault (Javelin is confident in what it delivers)

## 3. Name & Tagline

### 3.1 Name Assessment: "Javelin"

**Strengths:**
- Short (7 characters) -- works well at all sizes, from favicon to listing title
- Distinctive -- not a generic SaaS compound word (no "-ly," "-ify," "-io")
- Encodes meaning: precision, directness, speed, aim. A javelin goes exactly where you throw it. This maps to "describe what you want and it builds it." [insight]
- Memorable and pronounceable across languages
- Current app ID (`com.javelin.analytics`) is established

**Risks:**
- Multiple existing "Javelin" entities in technology: Javelin Technologies (3D/SOLIDWORKS, acquired by TriMech), Hexagon JAVELIN (manufacturing), and historical Javelin Software (1984-1988). [data] These operate in entirely different market segments (manufacturing, CAD, legacy financial modeling) with no overlap to Stripe ecosystem tooling.
- Domain availability should be verified: javelin.app, usejavelin.com, getjavelin.com, or javelin.ai are likely candidates. [hypothesis]
- Social handle availability (@javelin, @usejavelin, @getjavelin) should be checked on X/Twitter, LinkedIn, and GitHub.

**Recommendation:** Keep "Javelin." The name is strong, meaningful, and distinctive. The existing trademark holders are in non-overlapping categories (manufacturing/CAD software vs. Stripe App Marketplace AI builder). However, a formal trademark search in USPTO Class 42 (SaaS) should be conducted before any significant marketing investment.

### 3.2 Tagline Options

The tagline should reinforce the "builder, not analyst" positioning. It needs to work in the Stripe App Marketplace subtitle field (80 characters max) and as a standalone brand phrase.

**Primary recommendation:**

> **"Tell Stripe what to do."**

[insight] This is the strongest option because it: (a) positions the user as the one in control, (b) implies Javelin's embedded-in-Stripe nature, (c) captures the plain-language-to-action promise, and (d) is five words. It also creates a subtle contrast with every analytics competitor whose implicit promise is "learn what Stripe did."

**Alternatives (ranked):**

| Tagline | Strength | Weakness |
|---------|----------|----------|
| "Build anything in Stripe." | Clear builder positioning | Overpromises scope at MVP |
| "Your Stripe, your rules." | Empowering, operator-first | Slightly generic |
| "AI that builds in your Stripe." | Specific and accurate | Wordy, less punchy |
| "The AI builder inside Stripe." | Category-defining | More descriptive than memorable |

**Marketplace subtitle (80 chars max):**

> "AI-powered builder for custom workflows, dashboards, and automations in Stripe"

This is descriptive and search-friendly, suitable for the subtitle field. The tagline ("Tell Stripe what to do.") is the brand-facing phrase; the subtitle is the marketplace-facing description.

## 4. Tone of Voice & Messaging Framework

### 4.1 Voice Attributes

Javelin's voice is defined by four attributes. Each is paired with a "this, not that" clarification.

| Attribute | This | Not That |
|-----------|------|----------|
| **Direct** | "Here's your MRR dashboard." | "We've prepared a comprehensive analysis of your recurring revenue metrics." |
| **Capable** | "Done. I updated 47 subscriptions and rolled back 2 that had conflicts." | "Our powerful AI engine leveraged cutting-edge technology to process your request." |
| **Clear** | "I need to know: are you looking at all customers or just active ones?" | "To optimize your experience, could you provide additional parameters?" |
| **Honest** | "I can't undo a confirmed payment. Want me to issue a refund instead?" | "An error has occurred in processing your request." |

### 4.2 Language Dos and Don'ts

**Use these words:**
- build, create, set up, automate
- your (possessive -- it is the user's Stripe, user's data, user's business)
- done, ready, updated, built
- plain language, natural language, describe, tell
- workflow, dashboard, automation

**Avoid these words:**
- leverage, utilize, optimize, synergize, empower (corporate jargon)
- powerful, cutting-edge, revolutionary, game-changing (hype)
- simple, easy (patronizing -- let the experience prove simplicity)
- AI-powered, machine learning, neural network (in user-facing copy -- the user does not care about the engine, only the outcome)
- platform (when referring to Javelin itself -- Javelin is a tool/builder, Stripe is the platform)

**Jargon policy:** Javelin speaks in the language of the operator, not the developer. When referring to Stripe concepts (MRR, churn, subscriptions, invoices), use Stripe's own terminology. When referring to Javelin's own capabilities, use plain language. Never expose technical implementation details (API calls, database queries, edge functions) in user-facing messaging unless explicitly requested.

### 4.3 Messaging Framework

**Messaging by audience and context:**

#### Stripe App Marketplace Listing (Discovery)

The user is browsing the marketplace, evaluating whether to install. Messaging must:
- Lead with the problem they recognize ("Frustrated that Stripe can't do X?")
- Immediately position as builder, not analyst
- Emphasize zero-setup and Stripe-native
- Build trust through specificity (what it does) not claims (how amazing it is)

**Example listing copy:**

> **About:** Javelin is an AI builder that lives inside your Stripe Dashboard. Describe what you need in plain language -- a custom dashboard, a bulk metadata update, an automated workflow -- and Javelin builds it. No code, no setup, no data export. Built for founders and ops teams who know exactly what they want Stripe to do but don't have the engineering bandwidth to make it happen.

#### Onboarding (First Use)

The user just installed. Messaging must:
- Orient quickly -- what can they do right now?
- Provide concrete prompt examples (not abstract capabilities)
- Set expectations about confirmation and safety
- Feel like a capable teammate introducing themselves, not a tutorial

**Example onboarding message:**

> "I'm Javelin. Describe what you need and I'll build it. Try something like: 'Show me MRR by plan for the last 6 months' or 'Set up an alert when a customer's subscription is about to renew.' I'll show you what I build before anything changes in your Stripe account."

#### In-Product (Ongoing Use)

Conversational, concise, action-oriented. Javelin is a colleague, not a customer service agent.

- Confirmations: "Ready to update 23 subscriptions. Here's what changes. Confirm?"
- Completions: "Done. Dashboard created with 3 charts. View it now."
- Clarifications: "Did you mean all customers, or just the ones on the Pro plan?"
- Errors: "I can't access that data with your current permissions. Here's what to check."

#### Marketing / External (Website, Social, PR)

Confident but grounded. Lead with the user's problem, not Javelin's features.

- **Hero statement:** "Tell Stripe what to do."
- **Supporting line:** "Javelin is the AI builder inside your Stripe Dashboard. Describe what you need. It builds it."
- **Proof point structure:** Always pair a capability with a specific outcome. Not "AI-powered dashboards" but "Describe the dashboard you want and see it in seconds."

### 4.4 Key Messages (Ranked by Priority)

1. **Builder, not analyst.** Every competitor reads your data. Javelin reads it AND acts on it.
2. **Stripe-native.** Lives inside your Dashboard. No separate login, no data export, no setup.
3. **Operator-first.** Designed for the founder running their business, not the developer building it.
4. **Transparent and safe.** Everything Javelin does is explained, confirmed, logged, and reversible.
5. **Stripe-deep.** Not a generic AI -- tuned to Stripe's object model, edge cases, and billing realities.

## 5. Visual Identity Direction

### 5.1 Design Philosophy

Javelin's visual identity must navigate a unique constraint: **the primary UI is rendered entirely within Stripe's design system.** There is no standalone website, no custom CSS, no arbitrary visual expression in the product itself. The Stripe UI Toolkit provides the components, spacing, colors, and typography.

This means Javelin's visual identity lives primarily in:
- The **app icon** (128px+ square, displayed in Stripe Marketplace and Dashboard)
- The **marketplace listing** (feature images, screenshots)
- **External touchpoints** (website, social media, pitch decks, documentation)
- The **brand voice** (which IS fully under Javelin's control, even inside Stripe's UI)

The visual identity should complement Stripe's design language, not fight it. The brand should feel native and confident within Stripe's environment while remaining identifiable as Javelin.

### 5.2 Color Palette

**Primary Brand Color: Deep Indigo**

| Color | Hex | RGB | Usage |
|-------|-----|-----|-------|
| Javelin Indigo (Primary) | `#4F46E5` | 79, 70, 229 | Logo, icon, primary brand accent |
| Javelin Deep (Dark variant) | `#3730A3` | 55, 48, 163 | Backgrounds, depth, hover states |
| Javelin Light (Tint) | `#C7D2FE` | 199, 210, 254 | Highlights, secondary accents |

[insight] Deep indigo was selected for several strategic reasons:
- **Differentiation from Stripe's purple (#635BFF):** Close enough to feel native in Stripe's ecosystem, distinct enough to be identifiable as Javelin. Javelin's indigo is warmer and darker than Stripe's violet.
- **Differentiation from competitors:** ChartMogul uses teal/green. Baremetrics uses orange/coral. Neither occupies the indigo space.
- **Psychological associations:** Indigo signals depth, intelligence, and trustworthiness -- aligned with the Magician/Sage archetype blend.
- **Practical performance:** Strong contrast on both light and dark backgrounds. Works at icon scale. Visible in the Stripe Marketplace grid.

**Secondary Colors:**

| Color | Hex | RGB | Usage |
|-------|-----|-----|-------|
| Signal Green | `#10B981` | 16, 185, 129 | Success states, positive metrics, confirmations |
| Alert Amber | `#F59E0B` | 245, 158, 11 | Warnings, attention states |
| Neutral 900 | `#111827` | 17, 24, 39 | Primary text (dark mode / marketing) |
| Neutral 100 | `#F3F4F6` | 243, 244, 246 | Backgrounds (light mode / marketing) |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | 255, 255, 255 | Backgrounds, negative space |

**Total palette: 7 colors.** This is deliberately restrained.

**Important constraint:** Inside the Stripe App UI, you cannot apply these colors directly. The Stripe UI Toolkit uses its own design tokens (`primary`, `success`, `critical`, `container`, etc.) for the `css` prop on `Box` and `Inline` components. The brand colors above apply to the icon, marketplace listing images, external marketing, documentation, and any future website. The in-product experience inherits Stripe's color system.

### 5.3 Typography Direction

**For external touchpoints (website, docs, marketing, pitch deck):**

| Role | Font | Fallback | Weight |
|------|------|----------|--------|
| Display / Headings | Inter | system-ui, sans-serif | 600 (Semibold), 700 (Bold) |
| Body | Inter | system-ui, sans-serif | 400 (Regular), 500 (Medium) |
| Code / Technical | JetBrains Mono | monospace | 400 (Regular) |

[insight] Inter is recommended because:
- It is the de facto standard for modern SaaS and developer tools (Linear, Vercel, Notion all use it or similar geometric sans-serifs)
- It is free, open-source, and optimized for screen readability
- It has excellent weight and width support for a range of typographic needs
- It complements Stripe's own typographic choices without clashing
- It signals "modern, technical, trustworthy" -- aligned with Javelin's personality

**For the Stripe App UI:** Typography is controlled entirely by Stripe's design system. Javelin has no control over fonts inside the extension. This is fine -- it reinforces the "ecosystem-native" pillar.

**Typographic rules for external touchpoints:**
- Headings: Semibold or Bold, sentence case (not ALL CAPS, not Title Case For Every Word)
- Body: Regular weight, comfortable line height (1.5-1.6)
- No more than two font sizes per section. Restraint over variety.
- Code references in monospace, even in marketing copy

### 5.4 Iconography Style

**Direction: Outlined, geometric, functional**

Javelin's iconography (for marketplace screenshots, documentation, and marketing) should be:
- **Outlined** (not filled) -- aligns with modern UI conventions and Stripe's own icon style
- **Geometric** -- clean shapes, consistent stroke width, mathematically precise
- **Functional** -- every icon serves a purpose. No decorative illustration.
- **Monochromatic** -- single color (Javelin Indigo or Neutral 900), no gradients in icons

**Icon grid:** 24x24px base grid, 2px stroke weight, 2px corner radius on open paths.

**What to avoid:**
- Filled/solid icons (too heavy for this brand)
- Rounded/friendly icon sets (too playful for B2B tooling)
- Skeuomorphic or 3D icons
- Emoji or illustration-style icons

## 6. Logo Concept Directions

### 6.1 Design Brief

The Javelin logo needs to work in three contexts:
1. **Stripe App Marketplace icon** -- 300x300px minimum, square, 1:1 ratio (this is the most common context)
2. **Small favicon/avatar** -- 16-32px, must be recognizable
3. **Full lockup** -- Logo mark + wordmark for external use (website header, pitch deck, social profiles)

**Core requirements:**
- Must work on both light and dark backgrounds (Stripe Dashboard supports both)
- Must be distinctive at 128px (Stripe's minimum display size in the marketplace grid)
- Must not use Stripe's name, logo, or brand elements
- Simple enough to render clearly at small sizes

### 6.2 Concept Direction A: The Precision Mark (Recommended)

**Concept:** An abstract geometric mark evoking a javelin's trajectory -- a single diagonal line or arrow form that suggests forward motion, precision, and directness. Clean, minimal, geometric.

**Rationale:** Maps directly to the brand essence (direct, capable, immediate). A single precise stroke conveys both the javelin metaphor and the "one prompt, one solution" promise. At small sizes, it reads as a confident directional mark.

**Execution guidance:**
- Single geometric form, not a literal javelin illustration
- Diagonal orientation (lower-left to upper-right) suggests forward/upward motion
- Javelin Indigo (`#4F46E5`) on white, or white on Javelin Deep (`#3730A3`)
- 2-3 geometric shapes maximum
- No gradients in the primary mark (a subtle gradient is acceptable for the icon variant, as the current icon already uses this approach)

### 6.3 Concept Direction B: The Builder Block

**Concept:** A geometric mark composed of modular shapes that suggest construction and assembly -- squares, rectangles, or blocks arranged to imply building or creation. Evokes the "builder" positioning.

**Rationale:** Reinforces the "builder, not analyst" message visually. Modular shapes suggest customization and flexibility. Can be animated later (blocks assembling) for future motion design.

**Execution guidance:**
- 2-4 geometric blocks in a purposeful arrangement
- Asymmetric composition (not a grid -- suggests dynamic building, not static structure)
- Javelin Indigo primary, with potential for one accent block in a secondary color
- Must remain legible as a simple silhouette at 16px

### 6.4 Concept Direction C: The Prompt Cursor

**Concept:** A stylized text cursor or input prompt mark -- evoking the primary interaction model (type what you want, get what you need). A blinking cursor or command line prompt abstracted into a geometric brand mark.

**Rationale:** Directly references the product's primary interaction pattern. Every user interaction starts with typing a prompt. This makes the logo a functional reference rather than a metaphorical one.

**Execution guidance:**
- Abstract cursor or prompt symbol -- not a literal blinking bar
- Could incorporate the "J" letterform subtly
- Minimalist, single-weight strokes
- Risk: may read as "code editor" rather than "operator tool" -- must be designed carefully to avoid developer-tool associations

### 6.5 Recommendation

[insight] **Concept A (The Precision Mark) is the strongest direction.** It connects to the name, is the most versatile across sizes and contexts, avoids category confusion, and has the highest distinctiveness potential. Concept B is a solid alternative if the "builder" message needs stronger visual reinforcement. Concept C is the riskiest -- it could work brilliantly or read as a developer tool, which conflicts with the "operator-first" positioning.

### 6.6 Current Icon Assessment

The current app icon (`icon.png`) is a radial gradient -- a soft blue-violet glow emanating from the center on a dark navy background. This has atmospheric quality but lacks:
- A distinctive mark or symbol
- Legibility at small sizes (it reads as a blur/dot)
- Any connection to the brand name or product concept
- Differentiation in the marketplace grid (it could belong to any product)

**Recommendation:** Replace the current icon with a designed logo mark before marketplace listing. The gradient background style can be retained as a secondary treatment, but the icon must contain a recognizable symbol.

## 7. Stripe App Marketplace Application

### 7.1 Marketplace Listing Strategy

The Stripe App Marketplace is Javelin's primary distribution channel. The listing is the storefront, the first impression, and the trust gate. Every element must be intentional.

**Listing Name:** `Javelin` (7 characters -- well under the 35-character limit)

**Subtitle (80 chars max):**
> "AI-powered builder for custom workflows, dashboards, and automations in Stripe"

**Built by:** `Javelin` (or the founder/company name, depending on entity structure)

**Category:** Analytics & Reporting (closest fit; Stripe may suggest adjustment)

**About (1,000 chars max):**

> Javelin is an AI builder that lives inside your Stripe Dashboard. Describe what you need in plain language and Javelin builds it -- custom dashboards, bulk metadata updates, automated workflows, and more.
>
> Unlike analytics tools that show you what happened, Javelin acts. It reads your data AND writes changes, with every action explained, confirmed, and reversible.
>
> Built for founders and ops teams at growing companies. No code, no setup, no data export. Install and start building.
>
> Javelin is Stripe-native and Stripe-deep -- tuned to Stripe's object model, billing edge cases, and operational realities. Not a generic AI bolted onto a dashboard.

(Character count: ~550 -- leaves room for iteration)

**Key Features (up to 3, 300 chars each):**

1. **Natural Language Builder** -- Describe what you need in plain English. Javelin builds custom dashboards, automates workflows, and executes bulk operations directly in your Stripe account. No code required.

2. **Stripe-Deep Intelligence** -- Javelin understands Stripe's object model, billing logic, and edge cases. It gives you accurate, context-aware results -- not generic AI guesses from a tool that treats Stripe as just another data source.

3. **Safe and Transparent** -- Every action is explained before execution. All changes require your confirmation. Full audit trail of everything Javelin does. Undo single actions or roll back bulk operations.

### 7.2 Feature Image Guidelines

Feature images for the marketplace listing (minimum 1600px wide, PNG/JPG, under 10MB) should:
- Show Javelin's UI within the Stripe Dashboard context (required by Stripe)
- Use mock data, never real customer data
- Highlight the chat/prompt interface as the primary interaction
- Show a concrete outcome (a built dashboard, a completed bulk update)
- Use Javelin brand colors for any annotations or callouts overlaid on screenshots
- Include brief text captions explaining what the screenshot shows

**Recommended feature images:**
1. The prompt interface with a natural-language request and the resulting output
2. A custom dashboard built by Javelin, shown in the Stripe Dashboard drawer
3. A confirmation dialog for a bulk operation, showing the safety/transparency model

### 7.3 Brand Within Stripe's Design System

Inside the Stripe App extension, Javelin's brand expression is limited to:

**What Javelin controls:**
- Voice and tone of all text content (chat messages, labels, descriptions, error messages)
- Information architecture (how content is organized within views)
- The types of visualizations and data presented
- The onboarding flow narrative
- Prompt suggestions and example text

**What Stripe controls:**
- All visual styling (colors, typography, spacing, component appearance)
- Layout grid and responsive behavior
- Interactive component behavior (buttons, forms, modals)
- Dark/light mode appearance

**Brand strategy implication:** Inside the product, Javelin's brand IS its voice. The tone, word choice, and interaction patterns described in Section 4 are the primary brand differentiators in the product experience. This is why voice and messaging receive detailed treatment in these guidelines -- they are the brand levers Javelin actually has.

## 8. Brand Dos and Don'ts

### 8.1 Messaging Dos and Don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Lead with the user's problem, then your solution | Lead with features or technology |
| Say "Javelin builds" (active, concrete) | Say "Javelin empowers you to" (passive, vague) |
| Use the user's language (MRR, churn, subscriptions) | Invent new terminology for known concepts |
| Be specific: "builds dashboards and automations" | Be abstract: "transforms your Stripe experience" |
| Acknowledge limitations honestly | Overpromise capabilities |
| Show concrete examples ("Try: 'Show me churn by plan'") | Describe capabilities abstractly |
| Say "Stripe-native" to mean embedded in Dashboard | Say "integrated with Stripe" (implies external tool) |
| Position as builder (active, constructive) | Position as assistant or copilot (passive, advisory) |

### 8.2 Visual Dos and Don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Use the defined color palette consistently | Introduce new colors without updating guidelines |
| Keep the logo mark simple and geometric | Use detailed illustrations or realistic imagery in the logo |
| Show the product in Stripe Dashboard context | Show mockups with custom CSS or non-Stripe UI components |
| Use high-contrast text on backgrounds | Use light text on medium backgrounds (accessibility) |
| Leave generous whitespace | Crowd content to fit more information |
| Use the icon on solid or simple gradient backgrounds | Place the icon on busy or photographic backgrounds |
| Match Stripe's visual tone in marketplace images | Use a visual style that clashes with Stripe's design language |

### 8.3 Competitive Positioning Dos and Don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Define the category: "AI builder" vs. "analytics tool" | Attack competitors by name |
| Highlight what Javelin does that others cannot (build/write) | Compare feature lists point by point |
| Let the user discover the difference through product experience | Make unverifiable "best" or "fastest" claims |
| Acknowledge when a competitor is better for a use case Javelin does not serve | Pretend Javelin is the solution for everything |

### 8.4 Brand Name Usage

- **Capitalization:** "Javelin" -- always capitalized, never "JAVELIN" or "javelin" in running text
- **Article usage:** No article. "Javelin builds..." not "The Javelin builds..."
- **Possessive:** "Javelin's" is acceptable
- **Never say:** "Javelin app" (the word "app" is prohibited in Stripe marketplace names and should be avoided in general copy too)
- **Acceptable references:** "Javelin," "the Javelin builder," "Javelin for Stripe"
- **Prohibited:** "Javelin Stripe App," "Javelin AI," "Javelin Platform"

## 9. Competitive Brand Landscape

### 9.1 Brand Positioning Map

The following maps Javelin against its competitive set on two axes: (1) read-only vs. read-write capability, and (2) external tool vs. in-dashboard.

```
                    READ-WRITE
                        |
                        |
              [JAVELIN] |
                        |
    IN-DASHBOARD -------+------- EXTERNAL
                        |
                        |  [ChartMogul]
                        |  [Baremetrics]
                        |  [Coefficient]
                        |  [QuantLedger]
```
